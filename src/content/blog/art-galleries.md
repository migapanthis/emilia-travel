For my first weekend in Sofia, I decided to check out some of the art galleries in the city. Sofia is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, which means there are a lot of spectacular buildings, and it seems like most of these house art galleries and museums now. I chose to get a double entry for two, Kvadrat 500 (the national gallery) and the Museum of Christian Art, or the Alexander Nevsky Crypt Icon Museum. I chose this one mostly because it was in one of the most elaborate buildings, but also because these two galleries were right next to each other. 

Another gallery I considered going to was the Museum of Socialist Art, however it was a bit out of the way so I thought I might go another time. It was also renamed, previously being called the Museum of Totalitarian Art... 
Anyway, to get here I took the metro. The metro in Sofia is awesome, and heaps of people use it. I had to transfer once for this trip, but no drama!

First stop was Kvadrat 500. This is the national art gallery and seems to be kind of the hub for buying tickets and things. The lady tried to sell me the student price but I was scared she would ask for ID so I paid the full 18 euros. Probably should've just gone for it. Once I got into the gallery, it was not clear where to go. There wasn't really many other people there, and no signs or instructions, just this quite grand entrance area. Eventually I found where part of the gallery was, but that bit was pretty small and not so good so I went to the other side of the building and finally found the 'main' gallery. 

This art gallery was HUGE. I had no idea what I'd gotten myself into. It's in a huge building, I think it was a printing house or something originally? 
There are 3 floors (maybe even 4), each with about 6 (large) rooms of art, plus a sculpture garden. If you're going to come here, you should account for at least 3 hours I think. Make sure you have a drink bottle. Maybe even headphones (the lights are all on motion sensors, so I could hear them clicking the whole time I walked around). The highlight for me was probably the sculpture garden, lots of interesting sculptures there and it was a great day (not too hot) to explore outside. After this, I was MASSIVELY hungry. It was like 2pm already! All good though, Happy is just down the road. Happy is a Bulgarian chain that is very popular in London it seems? It is based on the Hard Rock Cafe, and is one of the places that we can get delivered for lunch at work that the company will pay for. It is sooooo good, and they have basically everything. 
After the waitress switched to English for me, she asked me how I was and I said "good, how are you?". She replied with "I'm at work, how can I be". Good answer :laughing:
I got pork gyros and a hugo spritz, awwww yeah:

![lunch](../../assets/art-pics/IMG_3401.jpeg)

Then, it was time to move onto the icon museum. I had no idea what an icon was before this, but now I know. Not really my thing. I didn't realise that Sofia had such a link to Christianity, but I guess that makes sense. This gallery was in the crypt of the Alexander Nevsky Cathedral. This building is amazing, and the gold parts reflect beautifully in the sun. 

![cathedral](../../assets/art-pics/IMG_3380.jpeg)

This museum was a lot smaller. Just one big room with a lot of icons and pieces of old churches and things. It was so quiet, but since it was in the concrete basement of this cathedral, every sound echoed. One lady sneezed and it sounded like a bomb went off. Here are some pictures from this gallery:

This was a great day out. I probably wouldn't recommend the icon museum, but maybe if you know more about Christianity it would be more interesting. Kvadrat 500 however, I highly recommend. Must visit alongside the Red Flat if you're in Sofia. Happy is also a must visit.
